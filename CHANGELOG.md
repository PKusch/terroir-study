# Changelog

What changed for someone studying with the app, newest first. The site updates
on every push to `main`, so dates are the day a change went live. Earlier work
is summed up in the README's roadmap.

## November 2026

### 28 November
- **Climate is now a visual page:** nine diagrams, each with the idea in a sentence and what to remember. They cover the 30° to 50° wine bands on a world map, Gregory Jones's climate bands with where Pinot Noir, Chardonnay and Cabernet Sauvignon do best, and a cool-to-hot switch showing what warmth does to sugar, acid, body and flavour. Monthly temperatures for Bordeaux, Dijon, Montpellier and Mendoza show maritime against continental, and their day and night temperatures show diurnal range. There is also a map of the cold and warm ocean currents with how a cold current makes fog, a vineyard you can move up a mountain, sunlight on slopes by season with cold air draining to the valley, and gravel against clay. Every number has a source, and a second reviewer checked the page before it went live.
- **Vineyard quiz:** 24 more questions, one or more on every climate diagram (reading the charts, doing the sums, and the why behind them). A second reviewer checked them; two were fixed before they went live. A button at the end of the Climate page, and every *Quiz › Vineyard* link, now opens the quiz on the Vineyard filter.

### 27 November (evening)
- **Viticulture** now covers the vine itself (species, clones, crossings, hybrids, rootstocks), its parts, and what is inside the grape, with a month-by-month chart of the vine's year for both hemispheres.
- **Vineyard quiz:** 40 questions on climate and viticulture in general, with their own filter. They were checked by a second reviewer before going live.
- Wine law: PDO wine is no longer said to be vinifera only, since a 2021 EU change lets member states allow some crosses.

### 27 November
- **Home page:** the app now opens on a home page with *Try it live*, a world map of the fifteen syllabus countries (and a closer Europe view), every country and region one click away, and the WSET Level 3 syllabus with where to study each part.
- **Principles:** a new tab with sourced topics on tasting, climate, viticulture, winemaking, wine law and price.
- Explore now has its own address, `#explore`.
- **Health:** a Principles topic on units, drinking guidelines and the health and social harms of drinking too much, with a units calculator. Every part of WSET's Level 3 syllabus now has a place in the app.

### 26 November
- Small project files: a Node version file, an editor settings file, issue forms for something broken and for ideas, and time limits on the GitHub workflows.

### 25 November
- **Weak-spot map:** *Show my weak spots* on the Explore map shades each region by how often you answer its quiz questions right, and *Quiz this region only* starts a quiz on the region on screen.
- **Tell them apart:** a Taste drill on pairs of grapes that are easy to confuse, such as Merlot and Dornfelder.
- All 124 grape profiles checked again; seven small fixes, and a test that keeps winemaking aromas (butter, toast, cedar) out of a grape's own profile unless its notes explain them.

### 24 November
- The 122 questions added since 20 November checked again by a separate pass: no wrong answers, nine weak spots fixed.
- More questions that ask why, not just what.

### 22 to 23 November
- **Service and food:** Styles cards for serving, storing, faults and food pairing, and 40 quiz questions with their own *Service* filter.
- **Spot the errors:** find the lines in a tasting note that WSET says lose marks.
- **Full 25-mark written question** in the shape of WSET's sample paper, with a model answer.
- **Full theory paper:** 50 multiple-choice and four written questions under one two-hour clock.
- **Recall it:** a map drill where you type the name of the region lit up.
- **Study plan:** a study streak, an exam countdown and a daily target, kept in the backup file.
- Lazio rebuilt from the three Frascati rule books.

### 20 to 21 November
- The last unchecked facts closed from official sources: Greek Reserve ageing, Maury, Colmar's rainfall, Crémant de Bordeaux.
- The first reasoning questions for the regions that leaned most on recall.
